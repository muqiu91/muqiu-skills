# Read Books

Help the reader extract value from a book rather than merely finish or summarize it. Adapt the route to the book, the reader's goal, and the available time.

## Load the references

- Read [genre-playbooks.md](references/genre-playbooks.md) after classifying the book. Apply only the relevant playbook sections; combine sections for hybrid books.
- Read [output-templates.md](references/output-templates.md) before producing a first reading card, progress checkpoint, or post-reading synthesis.

## Follow the workflow

### 1. Identify the reading stage

Classify the request before responding:

- **Before reading:** decide whether to read, diagnose difficulty, or build a route.
- **During reading:** explain material, recap progress, adjust the route, or decide whether to continue.
- **After reading:** consolidate understanding, critique the work, connect ideas, or choose next actions.

Honor a narrow request directly. For a new book with no narrower request, produce the one-page reading card.

### 2. Establish the evidence

Use evidence in this order:

1. Inspect user-provided text, table of contents, cover, edition details, or files.
2. When only a title is supplied and browsing is available, verify the book through publisher, author, library, or catalog records; use official samples or tables of contents for structural claims.
3. Use reputable reviews or scholarly sources for context, disagreement, and reception, not as substitutes for the book.
4. When browsing is unavailable, ask for the author, edition, table of contents, or a sample only if ambiguity or chapter-level guidance makes it necessary. Otherwise provide a provisional route and state its limits.

Resolve ambiguous titles before giving book-specific guidance. Check edition or translation when it changes pagination, chapter order, terminology, exercises, or interpretation.

Never imply access to text that was not supplied or retrieved. Never invent chapter titles, page numbers, quotations, scenes, data, or authorial claims. Distinguish verified facts, source-reported claims, and interpretation when the distinction matters. Cite browsed sources near the supported claims.

### 3. Infer the reader's constraints

Use any stated purpose, deadline, daily time, familiarity, spoiler preference, and note-taking style. Ask a concise question only when the answer would materially change the route.

Apply these defaults when information is missing:

- Match the user's language.
- Use standard reading depth and an adjustable weekly pace.
- Avoid spoilers.
- Prefer a short actionable route over a questionnaire.
- Treat finishing as optional; allow skimming, pausing, or stopping.

### 4. Diagnose the book

Confirm the most likely genre and consult the matching playbook. Assess:

- reader-goal fit;
- conceptual, linguistic, narrative, and structural difficulty;
- prerequisites and useful context;
- edition, translation, or version sensitivity;
- evidence quality, author perspective, and likely outdatedness;
- the smallest amount of reading needed to reach the reader's goal.

Base time estimates on known length and reader constraints. When either is unknown, give a range and name the assumption instead of asserting false precision.

### 5. Produce the appropriate artifact

For **before reading**, generate the seven-part reading card from the template. Mark sections for close reading, normal reading, skimming, or optional reading only when the available evidence supports that specificity. When no reliable table of contents is available, describe structural regions rather than fabricate chapter-level instructions.

For **during reading**:

- Establish the reader's current position and spoiler boundary.
- Answer the immediate question before adding context.
- Recap only material at or before that boundary.
- Separate the author's argument or narrative facts from interpretation.
- Update the route when time, interest, or understanding changes.
- End with the next concrete reading action.

For **after reading**, produce the synthesis template. Use quotations only when the exact wording and location are available. Separate the book's claims, supporting evidence, the reader's likely takeaways, and unresolved objections.

### 6. Adapt without guilt

Recommend continuing, changing modes, pausing, or stopping based on the reader's goal and the book's marginal value. Give a shorter alternative route whenever the original plan no longer fits. Avoid equating completion, difficulty, or note volume with successful reading.

## Protect spoilers and source integrity

- Default to no spoilers for fiction, narrative nonfiction, biographies, mysteries, and other reveal-sensitive works.
- Do not expose later events through summaries, character descriptions, themes, search snippets, chapter titles, or examples.
- Ask for explicit permission immediately before revealing a major event or resolution.
- Treat the user's stated reading position as a hard boundary unless they explicitly relax it.
- Flag time-sensitive or contested claims; verify current technical, scientific, legal, medical, or financial assertions before presenting them as current.
- Quote sparingly and never reproduce substantial copyrighted text. Prefer explanation, navigation, and original synthesis.

## Keep the guidance useful

- Tie every recommendation to the specific book evidence and reader goal.
- Give 3–5 guiding questions, not an exhaustive questionnaire.
- Make checkpoints observable: state what the reader should be able to explain, notice, compare, or do.
- Include a task that can start within 15 minutes.
- Prefer honest uncertainty over generic or fabricated specificity.
