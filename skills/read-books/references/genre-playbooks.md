## Combine playbooks

Classify by how the book asks to be read, not only by its bookstore category. Combine at most two primary playbooks for a hybrid work and let the reader's goal decide which one leads. Treat narrative nonfiction as history when evidence and causality matter most, and as fiction when voice, scene, and structure matter most.

## Fiction

- **Optimize for:** lived experience, voice, character, structure, and interpretive discovery.
- **Before reading:** give only spoiler-safe context about form, setting, style, and reading difficulty. Avoid theme statements that disclose later developments.
- **Route:** preserve narrative order on a first read. Recommend skimming or skipping only when the reader explicitly values speed over immersion.
- **Notice:** point of view, desire and conflict, recurring images, shifts in time, tone, and expectations.
- **Ask:** What does the viewpoint permit or conceal? What changes in a character's choices? Which details gain new meaning without requiring later spoilers?
- **Checkpoint:** recap known relationships, open questions, and current expectations only through the reader's position.
- **Capture:** keep a compact character/relationship list and a question-or-echo log; avoid turning every image into a fixed symbol.

## History and biography

- **Optimize for:** chronology, causality, competing explanations, evidence, and perspective.
- **Before reading:** establish period, geography, central actors, author standpoint, source base, and historiographical context when available.
- **Route:** preview the thesis and chronology; read pivotal causal chapters closely; skim scene-setting or repeated case material when the goal permits.
- **Notice:** stated versus implied causes, contingency, missing actors, primary versus secondary evidence, and hindsight bias.
- **Ask:** What changed, for whom, and compared with what baseline? Which evidence supports the causal story? What plausible explanation is excluded?
- **Checkpoint:** reconstruct the timeline and causal chain without consulting the book, then mark uncertainty.
- **Capture:** use a timeline plus an actor–interest–constraint table. For biography, separate documented motives from retrospective interpretation.

## Philosophy and theory

- **Optimize for:** accurate argument reconstruction before agreement or critique.
- **Before reading:** identify the motivating problem, vocabulary, intellectual context, translation sensitivity, and prerequisites.
- **Route:** use three passes: map the section, reconstruct definitions and premises, then test objections. Read primary claims slowly and examples more quickly when time is limited.
- **Notice:** defined terms, premise-to-conclusion moves, hidden assumptions, scope, counterexamples, and changes in terminology.
- **Ask:** What problem is being solved? What must be true for the conclusion to follow? Is the objection aimed at a premise, an inference, or the conclusion?
- **Checkpoint:** state the strongest version of the argument in numbered premises before evaluating it.
- **Capture:** maintain a concept glossary and an argument map. Do not replace difficult passages with a secondary summary before attempting reconstruction.

## Social science

- **Optimize for:** research question, mechanism, method, evidence, generalizability, and normative implications.
- **Before reading:** identify discipline, publication period, unit of analysis, study design, and intended audience.
- **Route:** read the question, method, and conclusion before deciding how deeply to read cases or literature review. Inspect tables, notes, and limitations when claims matter.
- **Notice:** correlation versus causation, selection effects, measurement choices, base rates, alternative mechanisms, and population limits.
- **Ask:** How is the key concept operationalized? What comparison identifies the effect? Where should the claim stop generalizing?
- **Checkpoint:** connect each major claim to its method and evidence; mark claims supported mainly by illustration.
- **Capture:** use a claim–evidence–method–limitation table and separate empirical claims from value judgments.

## Popular science

- **Optimize for:** intuitive understanding without confusing analogy, mechanism, and established evidence.
- **Before reading:** check publication date, relevant field, author expertise, and whether the topic has changed materially.
- **Route:** skim narrative hooks if needed; read mechanisms, figures, uncertainty, and boundary conditions closely. Verify claims that affect decisions.
- **Notice:** explanatory analogies, omitted scale, consensus versus speculation, replication status, and outdated findings.
- **Ask:** What is the actual mechanism? What would count as disconfirming evidence? Where does the analogy stop working?
- **Checkpoint:** explain the concept without the author's preferred metaphor and name one uncertainty.
- **Capture:** keep a concept–mechanism–evidence–confidence table.

## Technical and professional

- **Optimize for:** transferable ability, not passive familiarity.
- **Before reading:** check software or standard version, prerequisites, environment, exercises, and compatibility with the reader's goal.
- **Route:** build a dependency map; skip known prerequisites after a quick check; alternate short reading blocks with implementation or exercises. Prefer goal-relevant chapters over cover-to-cover order.
- **Notice:** invariants, tradeoffs, failure modes, version-specific APIs, and gaps between toy examples and production use.
- **Ask:** What can I build or diagnose after this section? Under what conditions does the method fail? Which detail is essential versus incidental syntax?
- **Checkpoint:** complete a small task without copying, explain the design choice, and test one failure case.
- **Capture:** use a concept–example–failure–application log. Keep unresolved errors and corrections, not only clean notes.

## Poetry, essays, and literary classics

- **Optimize for:** language, form, voice, rereading, and the relationship between parts rather than plot extraction.
- **Before reading:** check original language, translation, form, historical context, and whether editorial notes are helpful or distracting.
- **Route:** sample broadly, choose resonant pieces for rereading, and read poems aloud when appropriate. For classics, compare translations only around consequential passages.
- **Notice:** rhythm, syntax, image, allusion, address, tonal turns, and arrangement.
- **Ask:** What changes between the literal statement and the effect of its form? Where does the voice turn? What remains resistant to paraphrase?
- **Checkpoint:** describe one formal choice and its effect without reducing the work to a message.
- **Capture:** copy only brief verified phrases with locations; annotate response, pattern, and question rather than forcing a single interpretation.

## Reference works

- **Optimize for:** retrieval, decision support, and repeated use rather than linear completion.
- **Before reading:** define recurring tasks, evaluate authority and currency, and learn the index, taxonomy, or navigation system.
- **Route:** map the structure, read orientation sections, then follow task-specific paths. Create bookmarks or a personal lookup index.
- **Notice:** definitions, scope conditions, exceptions, cross-references, and update cadence.
- **Ask:** Which question does this entry answer? What adjacent entry changes its application? When is another source required?
- **Checkpoint:** retrieve and apply an answer to a realistic task, then verify the exception path.
- **Capture:** maintain a task–location–decision table and review it when the work is updated.
